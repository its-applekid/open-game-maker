# Game audio packs survey (free / cheap / permissive), 2026-11-01

Web-only survey, nothing downloaded, nothing bought. Excludes what the library already holds: all 144 Kenney audio
packs, Sidetrax "Complete Works" (128 products), Case Portman "Cute & Cozy UI SFX", the chiptune barbarian soundtrack,
and the existing VCSL and Freesound notes (B11/B12). "No redistribution as a pack" clauses are fine for us and are
noted, not held against a source. Licence lines quote the clause that matters; "verify" marks terms a store page did
not show (403/429 on fetch or the licence lives in a PDF inside the download).

Format: name — author/site — size — price — licence — covers — URL.

## Recommendation

Get the free, no-attribution tier first: the Sonniss GDC 2026 bundle and the 2020 archive year (the two largest),
FilmCow, the Tallbeard Music Loop Bundle and Junkala's 512 retro SFX plus his two chiptune sets; that is roughly 60 GB
of CC0/royalty-free audio with no credits to track. Second, add the CC BY music (incompetech, Scott Buckley, Of Far
Different Nature) and keep one generated `CREDITS` list per game, which the asset metadata standard (C3) already
provides for; Freesound CC BY sounds go through the same list via its Attribution List export. Spend money only on
Alkakrab's Mega Bundle (€19.49) or the next Humble × Ovani bundle when it appears (nothing is live this month).
Licence traps: BBC Sound Effects is non-commercial (RemArc), Smithsonian Folkways and the Great 78 Project are not
cleared, YouTube Audio Library and Bensound's free tier are written for videos not games, Fesliyan needs a donation
licence for anything commercial, Uppbeat/Soundsnap are subscriptions that don't fit the budget, xDeviruchi is
share-alike (fine to play in a game, not to remix into a closed OST), Kai Engel has left CC for new work, and
Abstraction excludes six named OSTs from game use. Nearly every royalty-free source (Sonniss, Zapsplat, Mixkit,
Pixabay, GDM, Ovani) forbids redistributing the raw files as a pack, which matters only if Game Maker ever ships the
library itself rather than the games built with it; Sonniss also bans AI/ML training on its files, so keep them out of
any generation pipeline's reference inputs.
